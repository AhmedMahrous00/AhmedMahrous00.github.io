I study learning and strategic behavior in decentralized financial systems. My research combines mathematical modeling, simulation, and empirical analysis to understand how incentives, information, and feedback shape these systems.

I am a Ph.D. candidate at KAUST, advised by Roberto Di Pietro. My work spans performative prediction, decentralized finance, stablecoins, market manipulation, and financial social media.

2026:

The reflexivity threshold: A phase transition for multi-agent performative prediction

Ahmed Mahrous and Roberto Di Pietro

NeurIPS 2026 · Accepted

When predictions change the data that future models learn from, interactions between learners can amplify feedback. We study a spectral threshold separating stable retraining from worst-case learning failure.

2026:

What prediction-market contracts reveal at launch: Trading volume and dispute risk

Ahmed Mahrous and Roberto Di Pietro

ACM International Conference on AI in Finance (ICAIF 2026) · Accepted

Using 802,863 Polymarket contracts, we study how launch-time information predicts trading volume and dispute risk. Volume predictions generalize poorly across platform changes, while rare disputes remain difficult to predict individually.
